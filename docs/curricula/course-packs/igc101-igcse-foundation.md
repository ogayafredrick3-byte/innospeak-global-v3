## Course overview

IGCSE Foundation is a comprehensive course that builds a strong academic foundation for IGCSE examination success. Covering core subjects and study skills, it prepares learners for the rigour of international qualifications.

### Intended learners

Students aged 13–16 preparing for IGCSE, learners transitioning to international curriculum, and anyone seeking a strong academic foundation.

### Prerequisites / entry requirements

- Basic English literacy
- Completion of primary education or equivalent
- Internet access for online learners
- Commitment to regular study

## Learning outcomes

- Explain the purpose, key concepts and vocabulary of IGCSE Foundation.
- Apply suitable methods and tools to a realistic task related to IGCSE Foundation.
- Produce a practical work sample that demonstrates the intended IGCSE Foundation competency.
- Evaluate the work against explicit criteria and recommend evidence-based improvements.
- Apply responsible, inclusive, safe and ethical practice relevant to IGCSE Foundation.

## Capstone project

Solve a realistic IGCSE Foundation task by defining the need, selecting a method, producing a practical deliverable, checking it against clear criteria and documenting decisions, limitations and improvements.

### Required deliverables
- Problem, audience or context statement with measurable success criteria
- Process notes and evidence supporting key decisions
- Completed course-relevant artefact, analysis, performance or report
- Quality review using the stated assessment rubric
- Reflection on limitations, responsible practice and next improvements

### Acceptance criteria
- The deliverable addresses the defined task and intended audience or user.
- Evidence demonstrates the course learning outcomes and is traceable.
- The work is checked against the stated rubric and success criteria.
- Relevant safety, ethics, privacy, accessibility and attribution requirements are addressed.

## Sample assessment brief

Solve a realistic IGCSE Foundation task by defining the need, selecting a method, producing a practical deliverable, checking it against clear criteria and documenting decisions, limitations and improvements.

### Questions
- 1. Explain the key concepts or principles relevant to IGCSE Foundation and support your explanation with a course example.
- 2. Apply an appropriate method from IGCSE Foundation to a defined task; show the steps, evidence and reasoning.
- 3. Produce a practical work sample or artefact that demonstrates a relevant IGCSE Foundation competency.
- 4. Evaluate your result against explicit success criteria, identify a limitation and explain a correction or improvement.
- 5. Identify a relevant safety, ethical, privacy, accessibility, attribution or quality consideration in IGCSE Foundation and explain a proportionate control.
- 6. Reflect on feedback and explain what you would improve in a second iteration of the IGCSE Foundation work.

**Assessor guidance:** Use the course rubric and stated learning outcomes to assess the submitted evidence. Accept equivalent approaches when reasoning and evidence meet the criteria. Treat this as an owner-authorised assessment draft; obtain specialist review before formal high-stakes, regulated or safety-critical use.

## Draft marking rubric

| Criterion | Weight | Excellent | Competent | Developing | Not yet demonstrated |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Subject understanding and accuracy | 25% | Explains concepts accurately and applies them precisely to the task. | Mostly accurate; minor errors do not undermine the result. | Partial understanding; repeated errors require support. | Evidence is missing or major misconceptions prevent the task being completed. |
| Application and problem-solving | 30% | Selects and adapts methods thoughtfully; handles constraints and explains trade-offs. | Uses a suitable method and completes the main requirements. | Method is inconsistently applied or needs substantial prompting. | Method is unsuitable or there is insufficient evidence of application. |
| Evidence and artefact quality | 25% | Complete, traceable, well organised and meets all stated acceptance criteria. | Usable and substantially meets the criteria; minor omissions remain. | Partly complete or weakly documented; important criteria are missed. | Incomplete, unverifiable or does not address the brief. |
| Communication, responsibility and reflection | 20% | Communicates clearly; documents limitations and applies relevant ethical, safety, rights or accessibility checks. | Clear enough for the audience and identifies key limitations and responsibilities. | Explanation or responsibility checks are inconsistent. | Cannot explain key decisions or omits material responsibility considerations. |

- Review the outcome and key terms before each lesson.

- Keep source citations, versions and working steps where relevant.
- At the end of each module, write what you can do, what remains difficult and what you will practise next.
